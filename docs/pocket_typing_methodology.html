Pocket Type Detection

Method: env_cluster  |  Files: mgr_src/mgr/pocket_typing.py, mgr_src/mgr/pocket_framework_env.py

Design principle: every threshold has a physical basis. The algorithm sorts pockets by maximum probe radius, forms initial bands where the gap exceeds the grid-spacing noise floor, then uses a multi-channel rotation-invariant fingerprint — built from spherical harmonics (SH) — to decide whether adjacent bands represent the same physical cage type. No count-based gates, no material-specific special cases.

1 — What are Spherical Harmonics?

Spherical harmonics (SH) are a family of angular basis functions defined on the surface of the unit sphere. They are the natural generalisation of Fourier modes to functions of two angles — colatitude θ and azimuth φ — and form a complete orthonormal basis for any square-integrable function on the sphere [1].

Each spherical harmonic is labelled by two integers: the degree ℓ ≥ 0 (analogous to wavenumber on the sphere, determining angular resolution) and the order m with −ℓ ≤ m ≤ ℓ. For a given ℓ there are 2ℓ+1 independent harmonics. In this codebase we use the real spherical harmonics (also called tesseral harmonics), which are real-valued linear combinations of the complex forms and are standard in computational chemistry:

Real SH definition:

Yℓ,m(θ,φ) = Nℓ|m|  Pℓ|m|(cos θ)  ×  ⎧ √2 cos(mφ)  for m > 0  /  1  for m = 0  /  √2 sin(|m|φ)  for m < 0

Normalisation:    Nℓm = √[ (2ℓ+1)4π  ·  (ℓ−m)!(ℓ+m)! ]

where Pℓm are the associated Legendre polynomials and ∫ Yℓm(Ω) Yℓ′m′(Ω) dΩ = δℓℓ′δmm′.

The degree ℓ controls the angular frequency: ℓ=0 is a constant (spherical average), ℓ=1 is a dipole, ℓ=2 a quadrupole, and so on. For this application we use l_max = 6, giving 49 angular channels (ℓ = 0…6, each contributing 2ℓ+1 values of m) which we then compress into a 7-element power spectrum as described next.

2 — The SH power spectrum and rotation invariance

Given a discrete set of k unit vectors {ûk} pointing toward neighbours (either framework atoms or adjacent pocket centres), we define the angular momentum coefficient at degree ℓ and order m as:

aℓm = Σk  wk  Yℓm(ûk)     (weighted sum over all neighbour directions)

The SH power spectrum is then defined as the sum of squared coefficients over all orders m for each degree ℓ:

Power spectrum:    Pℓ = Σm=−ℓℓ  |aℓm|2 = Σm=−ℓℓ   | Σk  wk  Yℓm(ûk) |2     ℓ = 0, 1, …, l_max

In code: power[l] = Σ_m |Σ_k w_k * Y_lm(θ_k, φ_k)|² — see sh_power_spectrum_unit_dirs() in pocket_framework_env.py:231

The output is a vector of l_max + 1 non-negative real numbers. This construction appears in the SOAP descriptor [2] and goes back to early work on angular symmetry functions [3].

Why Pℓ is rotation-invariant

Under a rotation R of the coordinate frame, the spherical harmonics at fixed degree ℓ mix only among themselves via the Wigner D-matrix:

Yℓm(Rû) = Σm′  D(ℓ)m′m(R)  Yℓm′(û)

The D-matrices are unitary: (D(ℓ))† D(ℓ) = I, so summing |aℓm|² over m gives the same result as summing |aℓm′|² over m′ — it is the squared Frobenius norm of the coefficient vector at degree ℓ, which is preserved by any unitary transformation.

Pℓ(R·{ûk}) = Σm |a′ℓm|2 = ||D(ℓ)aℓ||2 = ||aℓ||2 = Pℓ({ûk})     for any rotation R ✓

This means two pocket environments that are geometrically identical but oriented differently in space will produce identical fingerprint vectors, regardless of the crystal orientation in the simulation cell. This is an exact mathematical property, not an approximation [1,2].

Relation to SOAP

The SOAP (Smooth Overlap of Atomic Positions) descriptor [2] uses the same power-spectrum construction, but additionally expands in a radial basis {gn(r)} so that the full descriptor is a 3D object indexed by (n, n′, ℓ). Our fingerprint is a direction-only version (unit vectors only, no radial basis), which is appropriate because the distance information is captured separately in the radial histogram channels. The radial histogram is also rotation-invariant because it depends only on r = |û_k|.

3 — Multi-channel fingerprint construction

For each pocket representative, compute_pocket_fingerprint() builds a fixed-length descriptor from three physically-distinct channel groups:

Atom-environment SH

SH power spectrum of unit vectors from cage centre to framework atoms within the cage wall (dgrid < 1.5 Å, query radius 3.5 Å). Plus per-element radial histograms. 10–14 scalar channels per pocket pair.

Pocket PSD vector

Normalised probe-occupiable PSD histogram. Already computed during characterisation — zero extra cost. Encodes the size distribution of accessible void space within one cage. 1 cosine channel per pair.

Connectivity SH (weight × 5)

SH power spectrum of unit vectors from cage centre to directly connected neighbour pocket centres (MIC-wrapped PBC). Captures cage topology independently of wall chemistry. Counted 5× in the weighted mean — see §4.

3a — Surface void voxels and cage-wall atoms

Surface void voxels are those with dgrid < SURFACE_DGRID_MAX_A = 1.5 Å — physically, voxels that are close to (within ~one C–H bond length of) the nearest VDW surface. Framework atoms within SURFACE_ATOM_QUERY_R_A = 3.5 Å of any surface voxel form the cage-wall atom set. The cage centre is the dgrid maximum (the largest sphere that fits inside the pocket), which is more geometrically stable than a centroid for non-convex pockets.

Unit vectors from cage centre to each wall atom are computed and fed into sh_power_spectrum_unit_dirs(). The global (element-agnostic) SH power is computed once; per-element SH powers are computed separately for each atomic number present, giving per-element angular resolution. These are concatenated into the final fingerprint vector.

3b — Pocket PSD channel

The pocket PSD is a normalised histogram of probe-occupiable pore diameter (Å) from compute_psds_pocketwise_rust: random points inside the segmented region, local clearance on the distance grid, diameter = 2 × radius. Stored on each RAG node as pocket_psd_x_pbc (bin centers) and pocket_psd_y_pbc with psd_source = pocketwise_rust. If Rust fails, the pipeline falls back to a centroid-distance CSD on csd_x_pbc / csd_y_pbc (psd_source = missing_pocket_psd) — that fallback is not used in the ZIF-20 figures below. PSD cosine similarity adds a shape-of-voids channel complementary to the atom-environment SH.

3c — Connectivity-direction SH

For each pocket representative, we collect the Cartesian positions of all directly connected neighbour pocket centres (using minimum-image convention across PBC boundaries), compute unit vectors {ûk = (rk − r0) / |rk − r0|}, and compute their SH power spectrum. This channel encodes how many connections the cage has, and in which directions they point — its topological environment — regardless of chemical identity.

Only pockets with n_connections ≥ 3 are included (the SH power spectrum for 1–2 directions is not informative for rotation invariance since with fewer than 3 vectors the power is dominated by the ℓ=0 constant mode).

4 — Weighted cosine similarity

Two pockets a, b are compared by a weighted mean of per-channel cosine similarities. The cosine similarity for a pair of vectors (u, v) is:

cos(u, v) = u · v ||u|| ||v|| ∈ [−1, 1]     (1 = identical, 0 = orthogonal)

The final similarity is:

sim(a, b) = Σenv channels cos(ai, bi)  +  cos(apsd, bpsd)  +  5 · cos(aconn, bconn) Nenv + 1 + 5

Nenv ≈ 11–14 (depends on which element species are present in the cage wall). Connectivity channel omitted when n_connections < 3.

Why the connectivity channel is weighted 5×

In ZIF-20, the two large cage types at 7.26 Å and 7.61 Å are lined with identical 2-nitroimidazolate and benzimidazolate linker chemistry. Their atom-environment cosine is ~0.97 and PSD cosine ~0.94 — nearly indistinguishable from the wall chemistry alone. But their connection topologies are physically very different (different numbers and angular arrangements of windows), giving a connectivity cosine of only ~0.27.

With equal weighting (1 vote per channel), the two large cage types would have a weighted mean cosine of: 

(0.97 × 11  +  0.94 × 1  +  0.27 × 1) / 13  ≈ 0.91  → MERGE (wrong)

With 5× connectivity weight: 

(0.97 × 11  +  0.94 × 1  +  0.27 × 5) / 17  ≈ 0.75  → NO MERGE (correct)
ENV_CLUSTER_MERGE_COSINE_LARGE_GAP = 0.80   →  0.75 < 0.80 ✓

The weight of 5 was chosen as the minimum integer multiplier that correctly splits every known ZIF-20 large-cage pair while not over-splitting any of the other six benchmark materials (where connectivity is topologically identical within a type and the cosine stays above 0.80 after weighting).

5 — The watershed segmentation and what "window trust" means

The distance-grid (dgrid) assigns each void voxel a value equal to the radius of the largest sphere centred there that fits entirely in the void space. Watershed segmentation partitions the void into regions: each local dgrid maximum defines one region, and every void voxel belongs to the region whose maximum it flows toward (gradient ascent). The boundary between two adjacent regions is the set of voxels that are equidistant (in dgrid terms) from both maxima.

Each boundary is one or two voxels thick. The code stores, for each pair of adjacent regions, the maximum dgrid value found in their shared boundary layer — this is called the saddle-point radius and is stored in window_info["window_radii"].

Key subtlety: the saddle-point radius between two adjacent regions is not the same as the physical window diameter between them. Consider a ZIF-20 window pocket (max_r = 2.56 Å) adjacent to a large alpha-cage (max_r = 7.26 Å). The window pocket's own maximum is the relevant size for the window itself. The boundary between the window-pocket region and the alpha-cage region sits where the dgrid gradient changes sign — typically at dgrid values of 0.3–0.6 Å, because that is the lowest point on the dgrid landscape between the two basins.

This is why two different window-size thresholds are needed:

ConstantValueWhat it gatesPhysical meaning
WINDOW_TRUST_MIN_A 1.0 Å d₁-neighbor list
(legacy shape_refined)		 A connection is "trusted" for computing d₁-based typing only if the saddle-point radius exceeds 1 Å. This filters to connections between large regions that share a wide shared boundary (e.g., cage-to-cage connections through a genuine open channel). In practice, most window-pocket–cage boundaries have saddle points of 0.3–0.6 Å and are excluded here.
CONN_TRUST_MIN_A 0.1 Å Connectivity-direction fingerprint Any non-trivial adjacency in the watershed graph is included, even when the shared boundary is just a few voxels wide (saddle ≈ 0.3–0.6 Å). The 0.1 Å floor excludes only true zero-radius artefacts (adjacent regions that share a single voxel face at the framework surface with effectively zero void space between them). This gives us the full cage topology graph even in dense, small-window materials like ZIFs.

In other words: WINDOW_TRUST_MIN_A answers "is there a physically meaningful open passage?"; CONN_TRUST_MIN_A answers "are these two regions adjacent in the void-space graph at all?"

6 — Algorithm: initial bands → fingerprint-driven merge

Step 1: sort by max_r and form initial bands

All pocket representatives are sorted by max_r (the dgrid maximum — the radius of the largest inscribed sphere). A new band is started whenever the gap to the next pocket exceeds ENV_CLUSTER_INITIAL_BAND_GAP_A = 0.15 Å.

ZIF-20 example (104 pocket regions → 6 bands): max_r: 2.06 × 24 | 0.50 Å gap | 2.56 × 8 | 0.18 Å gap | 2.74–2.79 × 32 | 0.51 Å gap | 3.30 × 32 | 3.96 Å gap | 7.26 × 4 | 0.35 Å gap | 7.61 × 4 → bands A(×24) B(×8) C(×32) D(×32) E(×4) F(×4) → 6 initial bands

Step 2: attempt pairwise merges of adjacent bands

For each pair of adjacent bands, check two conditions:

  1. Gap condition: max_r gap between bands ≤ ENV_CLUSTER_MERGE_MAX_R_GAP_A = 0.50 Å
  2. Fingerprint condition: mean cross-pair cosine similarity ≥ threshold (depends on gap zone)
Gap-dependent cosine threshold: gap ≤ 0.15 Å → same initial band (no merge needed) gap 0.15–0.20 Å → TIGHT ZONE: cosine ≥ 0.90 (small gaps could still be grid-discretisation noise → demand strong fingerprint match) gap 0.20–0.50 Å → LOOSE ZONE: cosine ≥ 0.80 (gap is real but small enough that same-family pockets might span it) gap > 0.50 Å → NEVER MERGE (hard cut)

7 — Physical motivation for each parameter

ParameterValuePhysical basis & calibration
ENV_CLUSTER_INITIAL_BAND_GAP_A 0.15 Å At grid spacing 0.5 Å, max_r is quantised in steps of ~0.05 Å (the grid diagonal between adjacent voxels). A gap of 0.15 Å = 3 grid steps is a conservative noise floor: two regions with max_r differing by more than this almost certainly sit in different local basins of the dgrid landscape. Using a smaller value creates spurious bands from grid noise; using a larger value merges e.g. ZIF-20 bands B and C which differ by 0.18 Å.
ENV_CLUSTER_MERGE_MAX_R_GAP_A 0.50 Å Equal to one grid spacing. Pockets of the same physical cage type in the same crystal can differ in max_r by a fraction of a grid step (PBC image effects, small local distortions). Pockets from genuinely different cage types differ by at least one characteristic window size: in ZIF-20 the smallest real gap between adjacent bands is 0.18 Å (bands B–C), but the fingerprint check prevents merging those. The hard cut at 0.50 Å prevents merging across the large gaps (bands D–E gap is 3.96 Å).
ENV_CLUSTER_MERGE_GAP_THRESHOLD_A 0.20 Å Boundary between the tight and loose fingerprint zones. ZIF-20 bands B (2.56 Å) and C (2.74 Å) differ by 0.18 Å — in the tight zone. Their fingerprint cosine (measured) is 0.856, which is correctly below the tight threshold of 0.90. Placing this boundary at 0.20 Å makes the algorithm ask for a very high cosine match before merging pockets that are only barely separated in max_r.
ENV_CLUSTER_MERGE_COSINE 0.90 Tight-zone threshold. Set so that ZIF-20 bands B/C (cosine 0.856) are not merged, but genuine PBC-image copies of the same pocket type (cosine > 0.95) are merged.
ENV_CLUSTER_MERGE_COSINE_LARGE_GAP 0.80 Loose-zone threshold. IRMOF-1 has two cage types whose max_r values spread over a 0.36 Å range (5.63–5.85 and 7.18–7.54 Å) due to cell distortion. Within each type, cross-pair cosines are ~0.82–0.85, so 0.80 correctly merges them into two types. The ZIF-20 large-cage pair has a cross-pair cosine of 0.75 (after connectivity weighting), correctly below 0.80.
SURFACE_DGRID_MAX_A 1.5 Å The VDW surface is at dgrid = 0. Voxels within 1.5 Å of the surface sit in the cage wall region — they "see" the framework atoms and carry the angular chemistry signal. Voxels further from the surface (deeper inside the void) are in the cage interior and add mostly noise to the atom direction histogram. The value 1.5 Å ≈ a C–H bond length, capturing the first shell of wall atoms.
SURFACE_ATOM_QUERY_R_A 3.5 Å From each surface voxel, we collect framework atoms within this radius to build the direction histogram. 3.5 Å captures the bonded first and second coordination shells immediately adjacent to each surface voxel. Using a larger radius would include atoms on the opposite side of thin linkers (double-counting). Using a smaller radius would miss atoms bonded to the surface voxel's nearest atom.
WINDOW_TRUST_MIN_A 1.0 Å Strict gate for d₁-neighbor lists (legacy shape_refined method). Only connections whose saddle-point radius exceeds 1 Å are treated as "real windows". Physical windows in MOFs and zeolites typically have radii of 1.5–5 Å; the 1 Å gate filters out narrow boundary-zone contacts that are not traversable by a probe molecule.
CONN_TRUST_MIN_A 0.1 Å Loose gate for the connectivity-direction fingerprint. The watershed boundary saddle between a window pocket and its adjacent cage sits at dgrid ≈ 0.3–0.6 Å in ZIF-type materials, not at the window pocket's own max_r. The 0.1 Å floor excludes zero-radius artefacts (phantom voxel-face contacts at the framework surface) while keeping all genuine adjacencies.
FP_L_MAX 6 Angular resolution of the SH fingerprint. With ℓ_max = 6 the power spectrum has 7 components (ℓ = 0…6). ℓ = 0 captures isotropy, ℓ = 2 captures quadrupolar (e.g. tetrahedral vs octahedral) symmetry, ℓ = 4 and 6 capture finer angular patterns. Increasing ℓ_max beyond 6 does not improve discrimination in tests on this benchmark set and increases compute time.
mask_thickness 0.05 Å Framework atoms closer than this to the simulation box boundary are excluded from the segmentation mask (prevents partial atoms creating phantom dgrid maxima at the cell face). The default was changed from 0.5 Å to 0.05 Å because 0.5 Å removes too many voxels in small-pore materials (ZIF-20 window regions), leaving too few surface void voxels to build a valid fingerprint. At 0.05 Å only a thin layer of boundary-adjacent voxels is masked.

11 — Comparison with shape_refined (deprecated)

shape_refined

  • Primary signal: d₁ coarse bands (PSD peak diameter)
  • d₁ unreliable for ZIF-20 — all windows map to same PSD peak
  • Count gate: skip refinement if n ≤ 12 pockets
  • ZIF-20 large cages: count gate blocks typing → only 5 types
  • Bug: checked non-existent max_window key → all connectivity silent
  • No rotation-invariant fingerprint

env_cluster (current default)

  • Primary signal: max_r bands (directly from dgrid peak)
  • Multi-channel SH fingerprint: atom directions + PSD + connection topology
  • No count gates — all pockets treated identically
  • ZIF-20 large cages: connectivity cosine 0.27 correctly blocks merge
  • Bug fixed: _edge_trusted now reads window_radii correctly
  • Two-tier connectivity: loose (0.1 Å) and strict (1.0 Å) gates

Case Study: ZIF-20 — Pipeline Walkthrough

ZIF-20 (Zn(Im)₂, LTA topology, 4800 atoms, a = 20.5 Å) is segmented into 104 pocket regions. We do not know how many pocket types exist. The pipeline discovers them from the data.

Step 1 — Sort pockets by d₁ and look for gaps

Each segmented region has a d₁ value: the maximum probe radius inside that pocket (the radius of the largest inscribed sphere). Sorting all 104 pockets by d₁ and plotting them reveals natural clusters:

Five gaps exceed the 0.15 Å noise floor: 0.50, 0.18, 0.51, 3.96, and 0.35 Å. These split the 104 pockets into 6 initial bands:

Do the PSD shapes confirm these bands?

For each pocket, compute_psds_pocketwise_rust samples 1000 probe-occupiable points inside the segmented region (with PBC), measures local clearance on the distance grid, and histograms pore diameter (= 2 × local radius). The plots use the RAG arrays pocket_psd_x_pbc / pocket_psd_y_pbc (psd_source = pocketwise_rust) — not the legacy centroid-distance histogram used only when Rust fails. Twelve evenly-spaced pockets from the sorted list:

Two families emerge: window bands with mean PSD peaks near 4–6.6 Å probe-occupiable diameter, and cage bands near 14–15 Å (distinct from d₁, the max inscribed-sphere radius used for sorting). Band-averaged curves (same pocket-wise PSDs):

Each band has a distinct PSD shape. Bands 4 and 5 (large cages) peak at 14.62 Å and 15.12 Å, respectively, and remain distinguishable in this pocket-wise PSD view.

Step 2 — Compute a fingerprint for each pocket

The pipeline now builds a multi-channel fingerprint for every pocket. This captures three independent aspects of pocket identity:

  1. Framework environment — per-element SH power + radial density profile (angular arrangement of wall atoms)
  2. PSD shape — the full histogram from Step 1 (cavity interior geometry)
  3. Connectivity directions — SH power of neighbour-direction vectors (topological arrangement of windows)

One pocket's fingerprint, broken down

Consider pocket rep 26 (Band 4, d₁ = 7.26 Å). Its per-element SH power shows the angular texture contributed by each element around the pocket centre:

Each element contributes a normalized SH power spectrum (ℓ = 0…6). Hydrogen is mostly isotropic (ℓ = 0); C, N, and Zn from the cage wall show more power at ℓ = 4–6 (directional linker and node geometry). These channels are concatenated with the PSD histogram and connectivity SH to form the full fingerprint vector.

Band-averaged angular signatures

Aggregating the global SH power across all pockets in each band shows whether the bands have distinct angular environments:

Bands 4 and 5 look nearly identical — their environment cosine similarity is 0.97. The radial density profile tells the same story:

Connectivity adds an orthogonal directional constraint on top of environment+PSD and is still critical for robust merge decisions.

Step 3 — Connectivity fingerprint: the tiebreaker

Each pocket's connectivity fingerprint encodes the directions in which it connects to neighbour pockets through trusted windows. The SOD cage (Band 4, 6 windows arranged roughly octahedrally) has a different SH profile than the α-cage (Band 5, 8 windows):

Their connectivity cosine is only 0.27 — a dramatic contrast compared to the 0.97 environment similarity. The 5× weight on connectivity in the cosine formula ensures this one channel can override the env+PSD agreement.

Step 4 — Cosine similarity → merge decision

The pipeline computes the weighted cosine similarity between all pairs of band representatives: 

sim(a,b) = mean( cos(env channels), cos(psd), 5 × cos(conn) )

Result: Merges only consider adjacent bands (sorted by mean d₁). Adjacent-pair cosines are 0.77, 0.79, 0.87, 0.72, and 0.69 (Bands 4↔5) — all below the applicable merge thresholds, so no merges occur; the 6 initial bands become 6 final pocket types.

Connectivity remains an important directional channel (Band 4↔5 connectivity cosine 0.27) that stabilizes merge behavior when geometry-only channels are ambiguous.

All parameters are physically motivated, not tuned on ZIF-20:
